After I wrote the original piece on [Team Uncommunication](/blog/2014/04/21/team-uncommunication/), I’ve been thinking a lot on how this can be practically implemented.

While the true zen of uncommunication is still lightyears away, there should be practices that reduce the need for inevitably delaying overhead of team communication. Especially in those teams where people are often in different locations and time zones.

The conveyor belt has impacted many industries in a lot of ways. One of those impacts is that we often build teamwork based on individual skills. Let’s say we have a project to launch and promote a new feature. In order to accomplish it, we would typically need such roles as backend engineer, designer, frontend engineer (and maybe even a mobile frontend specialist!), marketing specialist… and likely a project manager!

All they need to do is to coordinate (aka “communicate”) themselves to get to a single goal. All while possibly being 8–12 hours away from each other. Waiting others to do their job. How exciting!

This typical approach is based on the premise that it is best to let specialists do their part of the job. Common sense, right? It might indeed have been the best idea if we had shipping deadlines that aren’t as tight, and the runways weren’t that short.

So, here is an idea described as a few simple steps:

## Make Projects Really Small

Bite-size, quickly shippable, clear, measurable goals. Ideally just a day.. or a few days long, because you will…

## Delegate Each Project To Just One Person

One and only one person. All included objectives to be executed and measured by that person. But how will that person accomplish such a cross-discipline endeavour? After all, hardly anyone has all the skills necessary. That’s the where the actual teamwork comes in…

## Make Every Team Member an Educator

Everybody has their strongest skills. But instead of pulling people with these skills into every project, project owners should instead go and learn from their fellow experts.

Instead of “can you please do this?” it should become “can you explain how this thing works?”

What this gives us is project ownership (good), continuous learning (great), and faster delivery (fantastic!)

> Give a man a fish and you feed him for a day; teach a man to fish and you feed him for a lifetime.
